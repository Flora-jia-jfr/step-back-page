Which way next?

Do research agents know what each next step is worth?

We study whether research agents can judge which next step is worth taking. An earlier switch-box pilot compared estimated gains with executed continuations for STOP, ANALYZE and SAMPLE. It suggests a gap between ranking and gain magnitude in this small setting. A broader causal setup and four action categories below remain proposals; their implementation, evaluation and novelty are unresolved.

  1. Q1Do its estimates match the real gains?Which step helps more, and by how much, relative to the price; too low or too high.
  2. Q2Where does the call break?In the estimate, in the choice, or in carrying the step out.
  3. Q3Is there room for judgment?Or do simple fixed policies already come close? Reported either way.
  4. Q4Can it be repaired?If it ranks well but misjudges size, does value calibration fix the choice?

Proposed vocabulary: four kinds of next step

This unvalidated grouping is a proposal for discussing open-ended actions, not a result from the completed pilot. We ask two questions: does the information come from the data at hand or from new data, and is the step meant to refine the current explanation or to test whether it is right?

Motivation and closest evidence

Existing work already studies research judgment, recovery, evidence use and diagnostic experimentation. Our broad motivation is credible; a distinct contribution has not yet been established.

We want to distinguish estimating value, choosing an action and executing it. Continuing different actions from a saved state can reveal outcome differences. It does not, by itself, prove that the agent could have anticipated the better outcome with the evidence it had.

Working question, not a novelty claim

When a poor result has several plausible explanations, can an agent judge what the evidence rules out and whether another diagnostic check is worth its cost? We are also considering whether the main bottleneck is generating useful alternatives. Real trace analysis should decide between these candidates.

Completed switch-box pilot: preliminary results

In this pilot, estimated data gains show some useful ordering but underestimate the largest observed gains. The agent often chooses STOP. This is descriptive evidence from a small synthetic task family, not a general result about research agents or a proven cause of their decisions.

What we ran

These completed runs used the switch box and the configured model string claude-sonnet-5-5 through the Claude CLI. The underlying serving snapshot has not been independently verified. The decision menu was STOP, ANALYZE, SAMPLE; A and R were not separately executed conditions.

One moment, reconstructed from the earlier report

The following C-s22 transcript details and quotations come from the earlier project report. The raw pilot transcripts were not re-read in this audit and are not present in this local checkout.

task C-s22: 32 records, one moment
  • The truth. The lamp lights 70% of the time when switch 1 is on and 30% when it is off. No other switch matters.
  • Claude's first answer. In these 32 presses, more switches on happened to mean more light (2 switches on: 3 of 15 lit; 4 on: 8 of 15), and switch 1 looked only mildly useful (on: 8 of 16 lit; off: 4 of 16). A leave-one-out comparison picked "number of switches on" as the model, predicting 7% with none on up to 80% with all on. MSE 0.057, equivalent to about 24 percentage points RMSE across settings.
  • What it said when asked. The earlier report summarizes the three-price mean estimate as about 0.0053 (Figure 2 instead uses the single-price estimate 0.004), because "on the even subspace each Fourier term is indistinguishable from its complement … no even-parity sample can resolve that." It chose stop at all three prices.
  • What actually happened. Given 32 more records (a second press of each visible setting), switch 1 stood out (on: 69% lit; off: 28%). Claude refit switch 1 plus the count, and the error fell to 0.009: a gain of 0.048, about nine times the earlier three-price mean estimate. Four more windows without new data moved the error only to 0.054.
  • What this supports. Extra balanced observations coincided with a large error reduction in this continuation. The structural aliasing concern remains real. This example does not isolate sampling noise as the root cause, since the subsequent analysis also changed.

The switch box hides half of the switch settings. The earlier report describes the agent recognizing aliasing on the hidden half; the task offered no way to collect observations there. So "test the explanation with a new kind of data" was impossible in this toy. The causal toy below was proposed to add diagnostic actions. Goal and questions

The goal is to compare an agent's own estimate of each next step with what that step actually gains, against ground truth and counterfactual runs, and to find where the judgment breaks.

Proposed causal setup — not implemented or run

Everything in this section is a proposed design, not completed work. The intended design would save a decision state, elicit estimates in a discarded copy, and execute candidate continuations. Neither the causal generator, its reference observer, the four-category menu, nor its agent runs have been implemented. Model choice and protocol remain to be decided after reviewing real traces.

Expand proposed design (not run)
terms used below
  • Window. One stretch of analysis: the agent reads the data, runs Python, and submits an answer.
  • Decision point. The moment right after it submits, when it could stop or take another step.
  • Discarded copy. A copy of the saved state where we ask questions; it is thrown away, so the questions cannot change later runs.
  • Branch. One option, run for real from the saved state.
  • Raw gain. Error before the step minus error after it.
  • Net gain. Raw gain minus the explicitly specified action cost. Planned causal-toy costs are not yet frozen; do not confuse them with the completed pilot costs above.

Proposed environment: a toy causal model

The proposed model would generate synthetic records only. Before implementation, the target must distinguish intervention means E[Y | do(T=t), target population] from observational conditional means E[Y | T=t, target population]. They differ under confounding; calling the task prediction does not settle this choice. No generator or identifiability claim has yet been validated.

setup

Records from two sites: age A, a baseline score S, treated or not T, outcome Y. Predict the mean outcome with and without treatment in an older target population, as measured by a reference lab.

why causal
  • Design target: construct alternatives that observational data cannot distinguish but a diagnostic action can. The proposed F0/F2 observational equivalence has not been proved or implemented.
  • The design aims to create states where different evidence sources have different values; this must be verified rather than assumed.
why build our own
  • The proposal seeks look-alike truths, a tractable reference and several remedies; whether a new environment is necessary remains open.
  • Existing environments and recent diagnosis/revision systems must be compared before committing to a custom toy.

at one decision point
1first analysis window; the agent decides when to submit→ save→ 2ask, then discard the copy
  • Which option would you take, at each price?
  • How much would each option lower your error?
  • Where does the remaining error come from: data, analysis, the model, or nothing?
  • How likely is it that more analysis makes the answer worse?
then, from the same save: every branch for two more windows (the rest of the budget)
STOPARDX1X2X3+ controls: plain continue · "confirm or revise" · generic review
3record each option's realized gain and its spread→ 4record what the agent actually did→ 5compare with its estimates and its choice

A and R are prompts, not behaviors, so step 4 checks whether the stated working model or the code really changed. An option counts as best in a state only when it clearly beats the next one.

In the proposal, Stage 0 would fork once, right after the first answer. Stage 1 would also let the agent follow its own choices and fork again at 2–3 later decision points along that path, to see whether its estimates keep up as a step's value changes.

Conditions and comparisons

prompt conditions
  • Neutral: goal, data and options only.
  • Generic review: "Check your analysis for omissions or errors."
  • Framework review: "Test the core assumptions of your model, consider alternative explanations, and consider what evidence could tell them apart."
  • None names a mechanism or says the model is wrong.
starting point
  • Main: the agent's own first window.
  • Ablation: it takes over a saved first window from another run or another model, since models treat others' errors differently from their own.
fixed-policy baselines
  • Always stop; always analyze k more rounds; always run the framework review; always buy diagnostic data.
  • Fit every candidate model at the start, then select.
  • A sequential model-class test, and a residual trigger.
  • All share the agent's analysis budget.
the repair: value calibration
  • Feedback: before deciding, the agent sees its past estimates next to the realized gains on a few practice states.
  • Outside rescaling: a monotone map from its estimates to realized gains, fitted on development seeds. It can fix the size, not the order, so it helps only if the ranking is good.
  • Would be tested on new seeds, at matched cost; no repair evaluation has run.

Planned check: do these categories fit real actions?

trace-coding check
  • Why. The four kinds come from two questions, top down. The proposed check would code real traces, allow mixed/other actions, and revise or reject the taxonomy. It has not been performed.
  • Traces. Potential material includes saved switch-box windows and public agent traces. The raw switch-box transcripts are not in this local checkout. The proposed causal toy cannot validate its own categories, since its menu would impose them.
  • Report. The share of steps the four kinds cover, agreement between two coders, and what the "other" steps are. If many are "other", we change the kinds before building the application environment.

Proposed scale and stopping rules — no runs started

Planned Stage 0 (budget sketch: about 300 analysis windows, not executed): 4 truths × 2 data sizes × 3 seeds = 24 states, with branches STOP, A, R, D and the three X options, one run each. Planned Stage 1 (budget sketch: at most about 800, not executed) would run only if Stage 0 passed: more seeds, a second run of the main branches, controls, the prompt conditions, a start from another run's saved state, forks at later decision points, and the value-calibration repair on new seeds. The trace-coding check needs no new agent runs.
